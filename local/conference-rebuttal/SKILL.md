# Conference rebuttal

Use this skill when preparing a reviewer response, rebuttal, author response,
discussion reply, or a factual note to an area chair. Establish the current
stage's format, word limit, identity policy, and rules for new experiments or
citations from supplied instructions or official sources before finalizing.

## Core workflow

1. Read the submitted paper, reviewer comments, and only the experiments,
   tables, figures, or references that can support the response. Keep reviewer
   material local and do not expose private reports, paths, or credentials.
2. Build a small concern map: reviewer claim, response status, exact evidence
   locator, uncertainty, and any bounded revision or experiment commitment.
3. Answer each substantive concern with a direct first sentence, the strongest
   available evidence, a short interpretation, and a precise revision location
   when applicable. Combine concerns only when they share both evidence and
   resolution.
4. Preserve the paper's claims, numbers, metrics, settings, and terminology.
   Mark missing evidence or a proposed additional experiment explicitly; never
   invent results, citations, reviewer intent, or future release promises.
5. Run a final pass for coverage, evidence consistency, scope, anonymity,
   tone, word count, and venue-specific formatting.

## Writing boundaries

- Use neutral concern headings or the venue's required format; do not impose a
  fixed `Q1/Q2` or greeting template when the venue does not require it.
- Be appreciative and firm without attacking a reviewer. A private AC note may
  state a factual mismatch only with a precise paper locator and evidence.
- Use small tables or bullets only for naturally parallel evidence. Keep the
  response primarily readable prose rather than a template-shaped form.
- Running experiments, editing the manuscript, and submitting the response
  require authorization for those actions; use authorization already given.
- Do not add external links or identity clues in an anonymous response unless
  the venue explicitly permits them.
